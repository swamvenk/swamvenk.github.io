Fan layout — 9 × Arctic P12 Pro A‑RGB

Matched to the D33's nine positions. Reverse-blade fans pull air through the mesh as intake with the pretty side facing in; normal blades push exhaust.

PositionCountBladeDuty
Bottom3ReverseIntake — warm under‑glow + dust‑filter pull
Front (with ATX PSU)2ReverseIntake — glow diffuses through the wood slats
Top (bracket)3NormalExhaust
Rear1NormalExhaust

9 × $25 = $225 at Bizgram. PST cabling lets each chain share one header: bottom‑3 on SYS_FAN1, front‑2 on SYS_FAN2, top‑3 on SYS_FAN3, rear‑1 on SYS_FAN4; AK620's two fans on CPU_FAN1 + PUMP_SYS1. Mortar's 3× JARGB_V2 headers take the light chains.

Save $32 — light only the intake: drop RGB on the 4 fans you never see (top 3 + rear 1 → Arctic P12 Pro PST $17 ea). 5 A‑RGB + 4 plain = $193 → total ~$4,582.

Aesthetic — wood accents + fireplace glow

  • Set every RGB to warm amber → deep orange (#FF7B2D → #FFB347), slow breathing ~8–12s — no cold blue/white, no rainbow chase.
  • The front 2 reverse fans sit behind the D33's wood slats → the whole front face reads as a glowing hearth grate; the bottom 3 bounce warm under‑glow off the desk.
  • Keep the ProArt 5070 Ti's dark, minimalist line visible next to the wood — its own accent light set warm too.
  • AK620 G2 is black with a wood cap — no RGB needed; it anchors the top, not the glow.

Clearance & build notes

  • RAM: T‑Force Delta is 46.1mm tall — AK620's default dual‑fan position clears only 43mm → raise the cooler's front fan ~5mm (59mm in single‑fan position). Cooler stays ~165mm, under the D33's 172mm limit. Do NOT swap to taller Corsair Vengeance RGB (~51mm).
  • PSU: Seasonic Focus V4 is 150mm (D33 max 160mm), native 12V‑2×6 → no adapter for the 5070 Ti.
  • Case: D33 fits GPU ≤435mm (ProArt 306mm ✓), cooler ≤172mm ✓, all 9 fan positions ✓.
  • Bottom fans under the GPU add glow more than cooling — expected, not a fault.
